Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the 10 most common questions regarding Tomato Study features, privacy, study methodologies, and troubleshooting.

1. Is Tomato Study completely free and open source?
Yes. Tomato Study is 100% free and open-source software licensed under the permissive MIT License. There are no subscriptions, no in-app advertisements, no telemetry, and no paid tiers. The full source code is publicly accessible on GitHub.
2. How does Flowtime differ from the classic Pomodoro Technique?
Traditional Pomodoro enforces strict countdown cycles (typically 25 minutes of work followed by 5 minutes of rest). In contrast, Flowtime counts upward from 00:00 without arbitrary cutoff bells. You work until you hit a natural pause, and Tomato Study automatically calculates a restorative break length proportional to your focus time (e.g., 5m break for <25m work, 8m for 25–50m, 10m for 50–90m, 20m for 90m+).
3. Does the application work offline without an Internet connection?
Yes. The core application — including both the Pomodoro and Flowtime timer engines, flip and digital clocks, OpenGL procedural shaders, and Strict Focus endpoint controls — operates completely offline. An internet connection is only needed if you choose to listen to the live background lo-fi radio from late.sh.
4. How does Strict Focus mode work, and is it safe for my computer?
Strict Focus mode temporarily blocks distracting domains (such as Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, Reddit, and X) by adding localhost loopback entries into your operating system's hosts file, bounded by clear marker comments (# --- START TOMATO STUDY STRICT FOCUS ---). When the session completes, pauses, or resets, the entries are cleanly removed and original hosts contents restored. It makes no permanent registry or network driver changes.
5. Will Privacy Screen Lock close the app or lose my timer state?
No. Privacy Screen Lock calls the native operating system workstation lock mechanism (equivalent to pressing Win + L on Windows, loginctl lock-session on Linux, or pmset displaysleepnow on macOS). Tomato Study remains running quietly in the background and preserves all session progress while you step away.
6. What encryption algorithm protects my settings and study history?
When AES-256 Storage Encryption is enabled in Settings, the application encrypts your configuration file on disk using authenticated AES-256-GCM (Galois/Counter Mode). The 256-bit encryption key is derived securely via PBKDF2HMAC with SHA-256 and 100,000 hash iterations bound to your machine identifier.
7. How do I activate Zen Mode?
You can enter Zen Mode instantly at any time by pressing Z on your keyboard or by double-clicking the background of the main timer window. In Zen Mode, all borders, control buttons, and mouse cursors are hidden, leaving only the glowing clock digits and celestial starfield.
8. Can I customize study intervals and break lengths?
Yes. Open the Settings drawer (S) to customize work durations anywhere from 1 to 180 minutes, short breaks from 1 to 60 minutes, long breaks from 1 to 90 minutes, and choose how many work cycles trigger a long break. You can also configure auto-start preferences so breaks and work sessions roll seamlessly.
9. Can I rebind the keyboard shortcuts to my own preferred keys?
Yes. In the Settings drawer under KEYBOARD SHORTCUTS, click on any keybinding button, press your desired replacement key on your physical keyboard, and click SAVE & APPLY. Changes are validated and stored immediately.
10. What should I do if I see an elevated permissions notice when enabling Strict Focus?
Modifying the system hosts file is an administrative action protected by operating system security policies. On Windows, right-click the launcher or shortcut and select Run as Administrator. On Linux or macOS, run the launcher with appropriate permissions (e.g. sudo ./run.sh). If run without administrative privileges, Tomato Study displays an informative in-app notice and continues functioning normally without crashing.
